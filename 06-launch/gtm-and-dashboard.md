# GTM Launch Plan, StreamLine (B2C)

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Feature | StreamLine Spotlight |
| Goal | Engagement |
| Launch tier | M, Targeted |

## Goal & Audience
- **Goal:** Engagement, The Spotlight Rail is for someone who already pays and has already opened the app.  Nothing about pricing or plans changes
- **Target audience:** The Wanderer - People in the app, who we want to stay in the platform longer

## Launch Tier
- **M, Targeted**, It reaches a lot of people but is not a new product. It's one row inside an existing screen, seen only by people who already subscribe. It is not considered large as there is no significant brand change. Navigation, branding and the algorithm all stay the same, so there's little to reposition in the market.

## Channels
1. **Owned: Layered In App messaging - First reveal of new row; Push notification to Wanderers with no play in 7+ days: "3 new Spotlight picks this week." ; Weekly refresh rhythm. A predictable "new picks every Friday" gives people a reason to come back.**
2. **Earned: 1 - Reach out Micro-influencers, as they can provide an authentic reaction-type approach, adding sentimental reactions and excitement. 2 - Curators going on podcasts or doing short interviews or quotes from the editor; 3 - Film community: Curators share weekly picks with film critics, newsletters and community spaces where hidden-gem viewers already gather.**
3. **Paid: Minimal paid spend. small win-back test aimed at Wanderers who cancelled or went inactive. Upload them as a custom audience on social platforms with a message like "This week's picks, chosen by people, not an algorithm." Only do this once owned channels are working, and cap the budget.**

## Enablement & Assets
Launch brief (one pager for everyone); Support FAQ; Curation runbook; Pick Calendar

## Ownership, Budget & Timeline
- **Ownership & budget:** Kerri Barton (PM) owns the launch and each phase gate.  Bart, Executive Sponsor owns the final ship decision.

Launch brief. Kerri Barton owns it. It's a one-page summary of what Spotlight is, who sees it, why, the test dates and the decision rule. About half a day, due two weeks before the test.

Support FAQ. Karima, Support Lead, owns it. It answers likely user questions, such as why some users don't see Spotlight and whether the picks are personal. About a day of work plus a 30-minute team briefing, live before day one.

Curation runbook. Kevin, Curation Lead, owns it. It covers how to edit the feed, the 15–20 title rule and what to do when the rail disappears. About a day, due one week before the test.

Picks calendar. Also owned by Kevin, Curation Lead: a rolling 4–6 week plan of picks. It's the only ongoing cost, at 6–8 hours a week, and that time must be formally assigned.
- **Timeline:** Phase 1: Beta. All four assets are ready by week −1, and the test starts once tracking is verified. For 28 days there are no external comms: weekly feed refreshes, support ticket reviews and a status note from Kerri.

Phase 2: Launch moment. The Exec Sponsor decides in week 5. If it's ship, Spotlight rolls out to all Wanderers in week 6, with a 5% holdout. The brief and FAQ are updated, and the picks calendar extends to eight weeks. The rail itself is the launch.

Phase 3: Post-launch. Weekly refreshes continue. Retention readouts come in weeks 10 and 18. A decision to scale or wind down follows at week 18. The Month 6 churn result arrives in week 26.

## Success Metrics
- **Metrics:** 1. Time to first play. This is the experiment's primary metric: the average time from opening the app to a play of 2 minutes or more, with sessions that end without a play counted as 20 minutes. Success is at least 30 seconds faster than Control with p < .05 at day 28. It shows whether the scroll-and-abandon moment is shorter.

2. Successful sessions per Wanderer per week. This counts sessions that include at least 2 minutes of viewing. Success is a significant rise over Control at day 28. Faster plays only matter if Wanderers come back more often, and coming back is what rebuilds the habit that keeps subscribers.

3. Repeat Spotlight use. This is the share of Wanderers who play something from Spotlight in two or more separate weeks. Set the target once you've seen week 1 usage. One-time use is curiosity; repeat use shows the trust the whole curation bet depends on.
- **Bad signal to watch for:** Fading interest. Spotlight taps are strong in week 1, then fall back toward zero by week 4. People tried it because it was new, not because the picks earned their trust.
- **Likely post-launch decision:** The most likely outcome is ship, then iterate on curation.
