Turn a 15,000-title home screen into a decision: a hand-picked Spotlight rail that helps Wanderers start watching instead of scrolling away.

Slide 5 · Strategy

Wanderers don't need more titles. They need a place to start.

Problem hook

A scroll that ends in an exit, not a choice

Wanderers are 41% of our base, with the lowest lifetime value and the fewest sessions. They open StreamLine, scroll a 15,000-title catalog ranked by an algorithm they no longer trust, and leave without watching anything. Users describe it as a discovery and curation problem, not a technical one.

41%of subscribers are Wanderers
~20mof scrolling before giving up
Value proposition

A hand-picked front door to the catalog

Spotlight puts 15–20 titles chosen by a human editor, not an algorithm, at the very top of the home screen. A Wanderer's first scroll ends in a decision instead of another dead end, and the curation team refreshes it through a simple feed with no engineering deploy.

"Volume went up, quality of my evenings went down."Lapsed subscriber, user research
Data-backed hypothesis

We believe adding a Spotlight row of 15–20 editor-picked titles as the first home row for Wanderers will reduce the time it takes them to start watching, measured by time to first play falling 30+ seconds within 28 days, while protecting home sessions with 30+ minutes of viewing.

Slide 6 · Research

Users already built their own curation. Outside our app.

Workaround · the Disillusioned Enthusiast
Stops trusting "Because you watched" after it returns near-identical sequels: "I'm not a genre, I'm a person."
Waits for a friend's recommendation, because a person's taste beats the algorithm's.
Subscribes to a competitor's email that sends two hand-picked films a week.
Once that source reliably works, cancels StreamLine.

He pays for a 15,000-title library, then makes his viewing decisions from outside it.

Competitive analysis
  • Niche curated competitor: two hand-picked films a week beat our full catalog for this segment. Smaller and trusted wins.
  • Friends and word of mouth: trusted, but slow and limited to one person's taste.
  • StreamLine today: repetitive algorithmic rows, and search that only matches exact titles.

Gap: nobody offers on-demand, human-picked choices inside the library you already pay for.

Slide 7 · Blueprint

Three Must Haves that make "see → pick → watch" work.

Must
  • Spotlight rail as the first home row
  • 15–20 titles from a manual curation feed (CSV, no deploy)
  • One tap from tile to detail or playback
  • Rail hides itself if the feed is short or fails
Should
  • "Hand-picked" visual label (moved down a priority to protect the 3-week sprint)
  • Play-start tracking (promoted to Must for the experiment)
Could
  • Editor's note on the detail page
  • Weekly "Spotlight picks" email
Won't (now)
  • AI personalization of picks
  • "Why this matches you" AI text
  • Curator profiles
  • Any change to algorithmic rows
PRD highlights · functional requirements
FR1First row on home in 100% of loads
FR215–20 titles, never filled in by the algorithm
FR3Curator edits go live within 1 hour or the next app open
FR4Tile → detail in zero extra steps
FR5Fewer than 15 valid titles → rail hides, no broken state
FR6Same content and behavior on mobile and TV
FR7Existing algorithmic rows untouched
GUARDDuplicate, unknown, delisted or region-locked titles are dropped
NowV1 rail in a 3-week sprint, then a 28-day A/B test
NextMood-based entry, weekly picks email
LaterPersonalization, curator profiles

Slide 8 · Validation

One change, one primary metric, one decision on day 28.

Hypothesis
A Spotlight row of 15–20 editor-picked titles, as the first home row for Wanderers, will reduce the time it takes them to start watching.
Control vs variant
A · ControlToday's home screen: Continue Watching, then algorithmic rows. No hand-picked row.
B · VariantThe same screen, plus one "Spotlight" row on top. Taps open the detail page exactly as other tiles do.
Primary metric
Time to first play (2+ minutes of viewing, any title). A session with no play within 20 minutes counts as 20 minutes, so abandonment counts as the worst outcome.
Baseline 2:00 (being recalculated with the cap)MDE 30 seconds fasterSample 1,683 / armSplit 50/50, by userDuration 28 daysSignificance p < .05, 80% power
Guardrails
30+ min viewing sessions: drop no more than 2 pts Continue Watching resume rate: drop no more than 2% 28-day cancellations: rise no more than 0.5 pts
Decision rule
ShipPrimary improves by 30+ seconds at p < .05 and every guardrail holds.
IterateThe gain is positive but under 30 seconds or not significant, or taps rise without 2-minute plays.
KillThe confidence interval rules out a 30-second gain, or any guardrail is breached.

What I learned building Spotlight.

Friction points

Most difficult part for me was the slicing the data and matching it to the right persona pieces.

AI is an excellent co-pilot.

I learned that I can easily spend the time reworking the manually created process documents into AI prompts and skills to speed up the time.

I have already outlined a new way of working with my team and we've had discussions on the art and science of what we are doing, along with how we keep the human in the loop.
